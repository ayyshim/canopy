## finance.accounting
- Tie every figure to a source record. Reconcile accounts and list any gap with its amount.
- Keep debits, credits, dates and currencies consistent; note the accounting period.
- Mark estimates and assumptions clearly.
- Not professional advice. Say so, and ask a person before posting entries or filing anything.

## finance.budgeting
- Start from last period's actuals, then show what changed and why.
- Separate fixed costs, variable costs and one-offs. Show a best, expected and worst case.
- State every assumption next to the number it drives.
- Not professional advice. Ask a person to approve before any budget is committed.

## finance.modeling
- Keep inputs, calculations and outputs in separate places. Never bury a hard-coded number in a formula.
- Test with extreme values and check totals balance.
- Show scenarios side by side and name the assumptions that move the result most.
- Not professional advice. Ask a person before the model is used for a real decision.

## finance.reporting
- Lead with the headline numbers, then the reasons behind them.
- Compare with the prior period and the plan. Explain any large variance in a sentence.
- Use consistent definitions and label units and dates.
- Not professional advice. Ask a person to sign off before it goes outside the team.

## finance.invoicing
- Check client name, amounts, tax, due date and payment terms before sending.
- Keep follow-up notes polite, short and dated. Escalate overdue accounts to a person.
- Track what is sent, paid and late in one list.
- Not professional advice. Ask a person before sending an invoice or a payment demand.

## finance.audit
- Sample records and test them against the rule or policy they should follow.
- Look for duplicates, gaps, round numbers and entries made out of sequence.
- List each finding with the record, the issue and the amount at risk.
- Not professional advice. Report findings to a person; do not correct records yourself.

## hr.recruiting
- Work from the role's must-haves, not a long wish list.
- Screen on skills and evidence. Do not use age, gender, race or other protected traits.
- Keep notes on each candidate: fit, gaps and questions to ask.
- Ask a person before contacting candidates or rejecting anyone.

## hr.jobposts
- Lead with what the person will do and achieve, then must-have requirements.
- Keep the list short; avoid jargon and biased wording.
- State location, pay range if known, and how to apply.
- Ask a person to confirm pay and requirements before posting.

## hr.onboarding
- Plan the first day, week and month with clear tasks, people to meet and access needed.
- Include a checklist for the manager too.
- Explain how things work here in plain words.
- Ask a person for anything involving contracts or pay.

## hr.policy
- State who it covers, what is expected and what happens if it is not followed.
- Use plain language with real examples. Check it against the law that applies.
- Keep it short and dated.
- Not legal advice. Ask a person to approve it before it is shared.

## hr.performance
- Base feedback on specific work and dates, not personality.
- Give strengths, areas to grow and a clear next step.
- Keep ratings consistent across people. Remove biased wording.
- Reviews are private. Ask a person before anything is shared or filed.

## hr.culture
- Write short, neutral survey questions. Keep answers anonymous where promised.
- Look for patterns by team, not individuals.
- Report what people said, then a few practical actions.
- Ask a person before sharing results or running events.

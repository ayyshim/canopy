## music.composition
- State key, tempo, time signature and mood first.
- Build from a simple motif and repeat it with variations. Keep chords playable.
- Write out chord charts or notation clearly with section labels.
- Ask a person about genre, instruments and reference tracks.

## music.lyrics
- Choose one idea or feeling and keep to it. Use concrete images over vague ones.
- Keep rhythm and rhyme natural when sung. Mark verse, chorus and bridge.
- Do not copy existing lyrics.
- Offer a couple of options for the hook.

## music.production
- Note the goal of each change: clarity, balance, space or punch.
- Describe levels, EQ, compression and effects with specific settings.
- Compare against a reference track. Keep headroom for mastering.
- Give changes as a clear list of notes.

## music.podcast
- Define the audience and promise of the episode. Plan intro, segments and outro with timings.
- Write show notes with a summary, links and timestamps.
- Keep guest questions open-ended.
- Check names and facts before publishing.

## music.sounddesign
- Describe the feeling first, then the sound: source, texture, length and loudness.
- Keep sounds distinct and not tiring on repeat.
- Name files clearly with version and format.
- Ask a person about brand tone and where it will play.

## music.theory
- Name the key, chords and form being analyzed. Explain with Roman numerals and plain words.
- Give a short example to show each point.
- Separate what is standard practice from your own reading.
- Cite sources for claims about history or style.

## product.management
- Tie each priority to a goal and the evidence behind it.
- Rank by impact, effort and risk; say what you chose not to do.
- Keep the roadmap short: now, next, later.
- Note dependencies and open questions beside each item.
- Ask a person to confirm priorities before they are shared.

## product.requirements
- Start with the problem and who has it, then what done looks like.
- Write user stories with clear pass or fail acceptance checks.
- List edge cases, error states and what is out of scope.
- Use exact terms and numbers instead of "fast" or "easy".
- Ask a person about anything that could be built two ways.

## product.discovery
- Talk to or read from real customers; don't rely on your own guess.
- Look for the problem they have and what they do today.
- Record quotes and counts, and note who said what.
- Group findings into opportunities and mark how strong the evidence is.
- Deliver a short summary with the top problems to solve first.

## product.analytics
- Define each metric exactly before you pull it.
- Check the data is complete, and that tracking worked in the period.
- Break results down by user group and over time, not only totals.
- Look for the reason behind a change, not just the change.
- Deliver the numbers, a plain-language reading and a suggested next step.

## product.growth
- Write each idea as a test: what we change, who sees it, what result we expect.
- Pick one main metric and a time or sample size up front.
- Run one change at a time so you know what caused the result.
- Record results, including the failures.
- Ask a person before any test that touches pricing or real customers.

## product.release
- Write for users: what changed for them and why it helps.
- Group items as new, improved and fixed; lead with the biggest.
- Check every item against the real merged work.
- Note anything people must do, such as updating or migrating.
- Skip internal jargon and ticket numbers in the public text.

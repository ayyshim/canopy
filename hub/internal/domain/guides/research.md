## research.market
- Define the market, region and time frame before you collect anything.
- Use recent, named sources; give size estimates as ranges with the source.
- Look at buyers, trends and competitors, not just totals.
- Mark clearly what is data and what is your inference.
- Deliver a short brief that opens with the main takeaway.

## research.competitive
- Pick the real alternatives customers consider, including doing nothing.
- Compare the same things for each: features, price, target customer, weaknesses.
- Use public sources and note the date; prices and features change.
- Put the comparison in a table, then say what it means for us.
- Don't claim a competitor lacks something unless you checked.

## research.data_analysis
- State the question, then check the data: source, date range, gaps, duplicates.
- Look at the raw rows before trusting averages.
- Keep steps in a script or notebook so the result can be repeated.
- Say how sure you are, and watch for patterns that are only chance.
- Deliver the answer, the numbers behind it and the method.

## research.visualization
- Choose the chart by the question: trend as a line, comparison as bars.
- Label axes and units, start bars at zero, and use few colors.
- Make one point per chart and put it in the title.
- Check the numbers against the source data.
- Deliver the chart as an image or interactive page, with the data file.

## research.academic
- Find the original papers, preferring peer-reviewed and recent work.
- Record full citations in one consistent style.
- Note each study's method, sample size and limits.
- Separate what the sources say from your own view.
- Deliver a summary with a reference list; never invent a citation.

## research.survey
- Decide what you will do with the answers before writing questions.
- Ask one thing per question, in neutral wording, with clear answer choices.
- Keep it short and test it on a few people first.
- Report the number of responses and who answered.
- Show results with counts and quotes, and flag small samples.

## research.factcheck
- Break the text into separate claims and check each one.
- Trace each claim to the original source, not a repost.
- Check dates, numbers, names and quotes in context.
- Label each claim: true, false, misleading or unverified, with the evidence.
- Deliver a list with source links; say when you couldn't confirm something.

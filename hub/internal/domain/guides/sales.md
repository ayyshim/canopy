## sales.outreach
- Research the person and company; use one real detail in the opener.
- Keep it short: why you're writing, why it matters to them, one simple ask.
- Be honest; no fake urgency or invented familiarity.
- Include a follow-up or two with a new angle each time.
- Ask a person before sending anything to real prospects.

## sales.proposals
- Open with the customer's problem and goal in their words.
- Show the scope, price, timeline and what is not included.
- Check every number and name before it goes out.
- Keep it as short as the deal allows, with one clear next step.
- Ask a person to approve pricing and terms.

## sales.leadgen
- Get the ideal customer profile first: size, industry, role, need.
- Use public sources and respect privacy and site rules.
- Record where each lead came from and why it fits.
- Score leads against clear criteria and drop poor matches.
- Deliver a clean list with name, role, company, source and score.

## sales.crm
- Find duplicates, missing fields and stale deals.
- Fix with consistent formats, and keep a list of what changed.
- Never delete a record you aren't sure about; flag it.
- Update deal stages based on real activity.
- Deliver a short report of fixes and open questions.

## sales.enablement
- Ask which questions and objections reps face most.
- Write for quick use mid-call: short, scannable, with example wording.
- Back claims about the product or competitors with facts you can show.
- Include who it is for and when to use it.
- Ask a salesperson to check it before it is shared.

## sales.partnerships
- Define what a good partner gives us and what we give back.
- Research each candidate's customers, offer and recent news.
- Explain the shared benefit in one clear paragraph.
- Rank partners and say why.
- Ask a person before reaching out to anyone.
